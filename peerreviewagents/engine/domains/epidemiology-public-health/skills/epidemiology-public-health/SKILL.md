# Epidemiology and public health

State the target population, sampled population, exposure or intervention, comparator, outcome, time horizon, and claimed estimand from manuscript evidence. Classify the design before applying design-specific questions.

Map recruitment, inclusion, exclusions, follow-up, analysis, and missingness. Inspect temporal ordering, selection into the sample and analysis, exposure and outcome ascertainment, comparable measurement across groups, confounding control, clustering, survey design, repeated observations, and loss to follow-up. For prevalence and incidence, verify the numerator, denominator, eligibility window, person-time where relevant, geography, and case definition.

Census and World Bank have no active adapters. Do not call `lookup_source` for them. For a consequential public-series question, record the exact provider, dataset, table, variable or indicator, universe, geography, unit, period, release or vintage, adjustment, estimate, and uncertainty needed for later verification. Ask the authors for a stable identifier or source extract when it is missing. Aggregate agreement cannot validate an individual-level model.

For causal language, make the implied contrast explicit. Assess assignment, timing, plausible common causes, selection, positivity or overlap, interference, post-exposure adjustment, outcome-dependent measurement, and whether the reported analysis addresses the main threats. For natural experiments, inspect the assumptions and diagnostics specific to the stated design. A plausible mediator or collider is a question to resolve, not an automatic error.

Check adjusted and unadjusted estimates, precision, missing-data handling, multiplicity, sensitivity analyses, and whether absolute and relative measures support the prose. Do not demand every possible sensitivity analysis. Evidence that can rebut a concern includes design-based assignment, a defensible sampling frame, validated and comparable ascertainment, prespecified covariates, negative controls, robustness analyses aimed at the identified threat, or calibrated noncausal language.

Use STROBE-linked items to locate needed information. Do not convert missing reporting into a bias verdict or checklist score. Do not infer causal effects from temporal sequence alone, treat representativeness as required for every internally valid estimate, or equate statistical significance with public-health importance. Do not upload participant data or conduct heavy reanalysis.
