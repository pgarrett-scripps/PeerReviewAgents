# Clinical interventions and diagnostics

Establish the design and the central clinical claim from exact manuscript blocks. Separate treatment efficacy, safety, diagnostic accuracy, prognostic prediction, and feasibility because each needs different evidence.

For a registered trial, locate the exact registry identifier before comparing fields. Use generic manuscript tools to find registration, protocol, enrollment, outcome, analysis, and harms passages. When available, call `lookup_source` with provider `clinicaltrials`, the exact identifier, and the manuscript block ID. Treat fuzzy title matches as unresolved. A current record is not evidence of the pre-enrollment or pre-analysis state. Ask for version history or a dated protocol when chronology matters.

Compare design, arms, eligibility, target and analyzed sample, allocation, masking, intervention timing, primary and secondary outcomes, measurement time points, analysis population, stopping, exclusions, subgroup plans, harms, and amendments. Preserve original wording beside normalized comparisons. A difference becomes a concern only when it changes the tested question, analysis flexibility, safety interpretation, or claimed population. A dated amendment with a credible pre-outcome rationale can rebut an outcome-switching concern.

For randomized trials, inspect allocation concealment, randomization unit, clustering, participant flow, missing outcomes, adherence or crossover, estimand or analysis population, uncertainty, multiplicity, and harms ascertainment. Lack of participant masking is not automatically fatal when masking is infeasible. Examine whether outcome assessment, co-interventions, or analysis could still be protected.

For diagnostic studies, identify the intended use, participants, index test, threshold, reference standard, timing, blinding, indeterminate results, and verification pattern. Check whether sensitivity and specificity use the same population and reference standard. An imperfect reference standard limits interpretation but does not by itself prove the index test is inaccurate.

Use reporting guidance as a question map, not a quality score. Distinguish not reported from not done. Evidence that can rebut a concern includes a dated protocol, registry history, allocation documentation, a complete flow account, prespecified analysis, comparable outcome assessment, or a justified sensitivity analysis.

Do not infer misconduct, individual benefit, clinical recommendations, or comparative safety from registration metadata. Do not request participant records or rerun the clinical analysis. Report inaccessible history and ambiguous records as limitations.
