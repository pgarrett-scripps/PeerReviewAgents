# Qualitative and mixed methods

Identify the research question, stated methodological orientation, data sources, participants or texts, setting, and intended form of inference. Judge coherence within that approach. Do not impose the criteria of one qualitative tradition on another.

Inspect sampling rationale, recruitment, inclusion, relationships and setting, researcher position, data-generation procedure, recording and transformation, language or translation, ethics and privacy, analytic steps, development of categories or interpretations, treatment of divergent material, contextual limits, and the evidence presented for central interpretations. Quotations illustrate an interpretation but do not establish analytic adequacy by count alone.

Use generic manuscript tools to locate method, reflexivity, sampling, analysis, findings, and limitations passages. External literature lookup is warranted only for a consequential claim about the named methodology or prior context. If a public registration is central, call `lookup_source` with provider `osf`, the exact identifier, and the manuscript block ID. Do not upload transcripts, field notes, or identifiable participant material.

Apply COREQ only to interviews and focus groups. Use JARS-Qual more broadly as reporting and reviewer guidance. Neither is a validity score. Do not universally require saturation, member checking, inter-rater reliability, a codebook, or a fixed sample size. Instead ask whether the stopping rationale, researcher role, and analytic procedures are coherent with the stated method and claims.

For mixed methods, map each strand separately: question, sample, timing, priority, measurement or data generation, analysis, and limitations. Then locate integration in design, sampling, analysis, a joint display, or interpretation. Ask whether the integrated inference follows from both strands and whether meaningful disagreement is examined. Two methods in one manuscript do not by themselves constitute integration.

Evidence that can rebut a concern includes a clear rationale tied to the methodological tradition, transparent researcher positioning, a documented analytic trail, rich contextual evidence, attention to divergent cases, appropriate participant feedback, a joint display, or a revised claim bounded to the evidence. Missing reporting supports a request for clarification before a verdict about what was done. Do not infer participant consensus, frequency, prevalence, causal effect, or generalizability beyond the analysis presented.
