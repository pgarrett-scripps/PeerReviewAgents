# Humanities and interpretive scholarship

This skill provides limited methods coverage. It does not replace disciplinary expertise, inspect restricted archives, authenticate artifacts, or claim comprehensive humanities support.

Map each central interpretive claim to the supplied primary or secondary source, edition, translation, date, archive or collection, folio or page, quotation, and manuscript block. Distinguish contemporary evidence from later recollection, primary evidence from secondary interpretation, and author translation from a published translation.

Inspect whether quotations preserve material context, whether edition or translation choices affect the argument, whether corpus selection is explained, whether provenance supports the source identity, and whether the conclusion exceeds the temporal, geographic, linguistic, or genre scope. Look for counterexamples the manuscript itself reports and assess whether they qualify rather than destroy the thesis.

Use `lookup_source` with provider `crossref` or `datacite`, an exact DOI, and a manuscript block ID for publication or DOI-registered edition identity. Both active adapters require a DOI. Metadata cannot verify a quotation, archival content, translation, or interpretation. Specialized archive endpoints remain unavailable until audited.

Evidence that can rebut a concern includes an exact edition citation, parallel original-language text, a documented translation choice, archive and shelfmark, a transparent corpus rationale, contextual passages, or a claim narrowed to the selected evidence. Do not infer archive absence from search failure, demand disclosure of restricted cultural material, or adjudicate authenticity from metadata alone.
